# Concept sheet v1 — Two Species. One Burrow.

**Channel:** Extinct Life · **Format:** 9:16, 35 s, loopable · **Language:** English · **Status:** idea for owner approval (`not-yet`)

## One-line idea

A synchrotron scan of a fossil burrow reveals an injured amphibian lying beside a sleeping mammal ancestor — not a predator and prey, but a shelter story sealed in rock for 250 million years.

## Working title

**Two Species. One Burrow. 250 Million Years.**

Alternates: *The Burrow They Shared* · *Why Did They Lie Together?*

## Hook (first frame)

Visual: a plain lithified burrow cast in portrait frame. One clean X-ray sweep crosses it.

On-screen text: `TWO SPECIES. ONE BURROW.`

Narration: “Inside this rock, two animals died together 250 million years ago.”

## The “Wait.” turn

Narration: “Wait — they weren’t enemies.”

The cutaway opens. Two skeletons appear side by side: a curled *Thrinaxodon* and a much smaller *Broomistega*. The expected fight never happens.

## Visual proof gates

- Synchrotron-style X-ray sweep over an original parametric burrow cast.
- True sagittal cutaway, not a flat overlay.
- Healed rib fractures shown as a labelled anatomical detail; no gore.
- Absence of tooth marks shown as a checklist, not as a dramatic claim.
- Newtonian flood/sediment motion; loop closes on the same cast.

## Claim discipline

Primary source: Fernandez V, Abdala F, Carlson KJ, Cook DC, Rubidge BS, Yates A, et al. (2013). *Synchrotron Reveals Early Triassic Odd Couple: Injured Amphibian and Aestivating Therapsid Share Burrow.* PLOS ONE 8(6): e64978. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0064978. CC BY 4.0 — attribution required.

Observed: two skeletons in one burrow cast; partially healed rib fractures; no tooth marks; curled posture. Interpreted: shelter-seeking, aestivation, flood entombment. Keep these separate on screen.

Do **not** say: “friends”, “roommates by choice”, “they protected each other”, or “scientists proved they were bonded.”
