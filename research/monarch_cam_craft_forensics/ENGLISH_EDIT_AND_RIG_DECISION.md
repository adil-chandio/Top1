Otherwise use grounded foot, adjacent limb and wall context—not an invented whole animal or disconnected bar.

### Exterior / locator constraints
- Tokay example, **left hind foot (pes)**. Mark the tracked digit consistently asIV in a small orientation locator only after source/view consistency check; the locator is unmeasured and must be identified as such.
- DigitI shortest/clawless, II–V claw-bearing; no five identical claws. Pes versus manus formula must not be mixed. Source: Russell/Delannoy/Garner2026 Fig1/2 actual prior inspection, documented in `TOKAY_ANATOMY_CONSTRAINTS_ROMAN_URDU.md`.
- Surface dimensions, pad boundary, exact proportions, claw pivots and LDT boundary remain unresolved; observed plate width is not a proxy for true adhesive area.
- No internal bones/tendons/vascular systems modeled or asserted merely to make the shot seem sophisticated. External visual plausibility and scientific source grounding are separate requirements.
- Exterior realism can only be claimed for details actually supported and checked. Schematic label on the insert does not excuse contradictory exterior claws, side, connectivity or contact.

### Mechanism insert constraints
- Continuous connected digit surface, active distal tip lift, release progressing proximally, then visible clearance. Qualitative motion, slowed for explanation; no measured biological duration, joint-angle graph, fixed130degree command or universal20ms value.
- Any deformation links are animation controls, not a claimed phalangeal count. No tiny accordion segments presented as actual bones.
- Contact tint is an illustrative cue, **not measured setal contact, pressure, stress or anatomical pad demarcation**. Geometry and visible gap must tell the story without the tint.
- Keep claw distinct from the pad; omit fine claw mechanics only if composition/label makes its limited explanatory scope honest. Do not clip off an inconvenient digit and then claim full anatomy QC.
- Do not claim whole-foot load transfer or natural replant from an isolated digit demonstration. Payoff can show the released toe clearly in the same foot context without simulating an entire stride.
