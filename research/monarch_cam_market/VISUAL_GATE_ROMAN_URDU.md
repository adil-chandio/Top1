## Factual claims lock — precise denominators

Source: Pitman et al., DOI 10.1111/mms.12343, primary PDF above.

| Claim safe to develop | Denominator/caveat | Jo nahi kehna |
|---|---|---|
| Review includes 115 humpback/killer-whale interactions | Collected accounts, not random sample of all ocean encounters | 115 universal controlled experiments |
| Humpbacks initiated 57% of interactions with known initiator | n=72, not 115 | Humpbacks start 57% of every encounter worldwide |
| Killer whales classified in the review were 95% mammal-eating | n=108 | All orcas eat mammals |
| Where mammal-eating killer whales approached humpbacks, attacks targeted calves | Approach subset n=27; attacked 85% of this subset | Every orca attack anywhere targets only calves |
| When humpbacks interacted with attacking mammal-eating killer whales, 89% of prey were other species | Specific observed subset, not rescue success rate | 89% rescue success |
| Mobbing sometimes allowed prey escape | No overall success-rate claim here | Humpbacks always save the prey |
| Response to attack vocalizations proposed; interspecific altruism not ruled out | Hypotheses/interpretations, not proved emotional motive | They knowingly rescue strangers out of human-like kindness |

## Encounter identity traps

- 2022 Canadian named whale Valiant ko 2021 Australian headline `Valiant humpback fights off four-hour orca attack` se mix nahi karna: latter `valiant` adjective hai, automatically same named animal nahi.
- Orcas attacking a humpback, humpbacks feeding, breaching, aur humpback interrupting an attack on another species chaar different behaviours hain.
- Valiant report by itself kisi third species ke rescue ka footage establish nahi karta.
- Norway stock ko Bigg's mammal-eating population ya Antarctica study event ka actual visual nahi label karna.
