## Important design constraints established by TEXT, not watched motion

- The paper distinguishes level walking, where distal adhesive sectors remain hyperextended, from climbing, where pads are deployed and then hyperextended away before ankle plantar flexion. **Do not animate a flat-ground walking clip as evidence for the full attach–release sequence.**
- Keep distal digit release, ankle flexion/extension, and pedal orientation conceptually separate. Do not make one foot-root rotation automatically stand in for all three.
- The paper describes markedly different left/right hindfoot orientations in static clinging with the thigh/crus remaining against the surface. The illustrated148°/29° angles are a particular static example, not release commands or approved keyframes.
- Forefoot reorientation is more restricted than hindfoot reorientation in the described comparisons; do not swap manus geometry or controls into a pes shot without acknowledgement.
- Structural ankle differences are not direct dynamic validation. The article itself calls for future functional studies of element motion.
