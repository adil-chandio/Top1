## Narration

A gecko can hold onto a wall. So how does it let go with every step?

The secret isn't just a strong grip. It's how that grip is controlled.

Under a Tokay gecko's toes are tiny, hair-like structures called setae. Their branching tips make extremely close contact with the surface.

They aren't suction cups. At that tiny scale, van der Waals forces help those tips adhere.

But direction matters. A pull toward the base of the toe, along the surface, helps support the grip.

To release, a gecko can curl its toes away from the surface. This movement helps the foot detach.

That toe movement is called digital hyperextension. Don't confuse it with the angles of the microscopic hairs beneath it.

So a gecko doesn't simply yank its foot off the wall. Close contact, the direction of loading, and controlled toe movement work together to make gripping—and letting go—possible.

## Science decision

- [Tian et al. 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1748224/) describes Tokay toe-pad hierarchy, tiny contact tips, van der Waals forces and a model for toe rolling and spatula detachment. It is a model, not a direct observation proving every detail of a toe-scale release front.
- [Autumn et al. 2006](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/209/18/3569/16118/Frictional-adhesion-a-new-angle-on-gecko) reports shear-dependent adhesion in isolated arrays and live toes; it rejects a whole-toe tape-peeling mechanism as an explanation of its findings. It describes hyperextension, but that does not prove the earlier wording's universal tip-to-base contact sequence.
- [Higham and Russell 2025](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.247980), a review, depicts active digital hyperextension at the end of stance and differentiates multi-scale structures. Therefore use a qualitative curl and avoid claiming an experimentally proven contact-front direction or a universal toe-joint release angle. Visuals remain illustrative, not a validated force simulation. This is an editorial source review, not independent expert certification.
- Change from v1: replace its two absolute sentences claiming tip-first, progressive tip-to-base contact breaking with the two qualified release sentences above. All other narration is unchanged.
