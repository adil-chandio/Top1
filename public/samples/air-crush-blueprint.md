## Shot-by-shot plan

Use the same brushed, copper-coloured aluminium can and open silver lid throughout. Use an amber/steel-blue lighting contrast on the two stations. Keep the lower frame clear for kinetic Roman Urdu phrases; no progress bar, persistent corner titles or caption panels. Keep the screen free of text during the replay. Warm specks are vapor tracers, cooler specks indicate air, and translucent beads are water. The tracers and force vectors are explanatory graphics, not literal visible gas.

## Editable production prompt

> Plan a portrait 3D explainer about why atmospheric pressure can buckle a can after steam condenses. Write conversational Roman Urdu captions and exact narration. Open on the result before explaining it. Stay with one unbranded prop, swapping between macro exterior views and an accurate axial cutaway. Show steam displacing most of the air first, the open mouth under water next, and droplets forming as vapor condenses. Keep the exterior pressure indicator steady when the inside indicator falls. Replay the snap with asymmetric angular creases, not a uniform squash. Keep the rims rigid and the surface label attached. Leave the replay clean of text. Leave a short gap in narration for the metal and water sounds, then land on “Can ko hawa ne kuchla.” Label hypothetical tracers and the pressure comparison. Give a shot list with timecodes, camera direction, scene motion, captions and Foley. No numerical pressure readings unless sourced. Explain the demonstration rather than giving a home procedure.

**What this sample can demonstrate:** timing, visual clarity and a complete, original camera-to-edit workflow. No claim that it duplicates a studio pipeline, simulates real pressure/fluids numerically, or is guaranteed to go viral.

**Physics check:** [UW–Madison Wonders of Physics](https://wonders.physics.wisc.edu/collapsing-can/) and [Science Is Fun, University of Wisconsin](http://scifun.chem.wisc.edu/HomeExpts/COLLAPSE.html). Steam replaces much of the air; cold water seals the open mouth and rapidly cools the vapor. The vapor condenses, so inside pressure drops; the larger outside pressure buckles the shell. Some water can enter; not enough to fill the can before it collapses. Timing is expanded in the animation to make these stages readable. This is a visualization, not a procedure for trying it at home.
