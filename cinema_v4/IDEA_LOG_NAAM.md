# IDEA · "NAAM LO" — the five people who are ruining your life

**Desi audience, world standard.** Not a Pakistan film. Not an India film. The one sentence every
child from Karachi to Delhi to Dhaka to Dubai to Southall has heard since they were six:

> **"Log kya kahenge."**

---

## The turn that makes it not a lecture

The film does not tell you to ignore them. It **asks you to name them.**

> *Paanch naam lo. Un logon ke. Jo tumhari zindagi chalate hain.*

Nobody can. Not five, not three, not one. And in that silence, the whole architecture of a desi
life collapses — because the audience you have been performing for **has no members**.

That is the entire film. One question the viewer answers in his own head, fails, and then writes in
the comments. **The comment section becomes the proof of the idea.**

**The one-line pitch:** *name the five people you're living for.*

## The seven moves

1. **HOOK — the task.** `Ek kaam hai. Kaaghaz lo. Paanch naam likho. Un logon ke, jinke liye tum apni
   zindagi jee rahe ho.` First two lines are a question and an instruction. Nothing else.
2. **The pencil doesn't move.** He sits. He thinks. He writes one name, crosses it out. That is beat
   two, and it is silent. **The failure is the content.**
3. **The receipts.** Not a list of feelings — a list of **prices**. The degree he didn't want. The
   wedding he paid for. The hair he didn't cut. The city he didn't move to. The job he took because
   *"log kya kahenge"*. Each one a thing, on screen, with a number.
4. **The count.** How many decisions. Then the question nobody asks: *un paanch logon ke naam kya
   hain?* Nobody names them because **they were never people.** They were a sentence.
5. **The reveal (awe).** `Log` isn't a crowd. It's a word your family used because naming a person
   would have meant arguing with him. `Log` is not an audience, it's a **shield.**
   → This is the eye-opening beat. It re-reads every fight of the viewer's childhood.
6. **The one time they really came.** At the funeral, at the wedding, they came for the plate, not for
   the person. (His own observation, kept, widened so it is not one community's ritual.)
7. **MORAL.** `Jinke naam tum nahi le sakte, unke naam pe tum apni zindagi nahi de sakte.`

---

## The moral at TABAHI level

> **Jinke naam tum nahi le sakte, unke naam pe tum apni zindagi nahi de sakte.**

Short enough to be a caption. Brutal enough to be quoted. And it is **arithmetic**, not comfort:
no names → no debt. The film never says "be yourself". It removes the creditor.

A shape other creators can copy without permission. That is a trend.

## Craft rules (world bar, not desi bar)

1. **Sentence length 12–14 words.** The gate enforces it. No fragment-stacking.
2. **You/I near 1.9.** The film is about *the sentence*, not about scolding the viewer.
3. **Four questions minimum**, all answered inside the film.
4. **One object** carries everything: the piece of paper with crossed-out names. It opens the film
   and it closes it.
5. **No music under the pencil.** Only graphite, breath, and a room.
6. **Works muted.** Every beat readable with sound off — captions in Roman Urdu, two words max.
7. **Every fact checkable.** No invented statistics. Prices, degrees, names of things — not numbers
   I made up. If a number is needed and I don't have it, I ask or I cut it.
