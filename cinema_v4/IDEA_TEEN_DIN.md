# IDEA · "TEEN DIN" — the first three days after you die

**This is an idea, not a script.** No lines yet. Read it, then kill it or green-light it.

---

## The idea in one breath

**Teen din. Utna hi waqt milta hai. Aur usme tum khud nahi hote.**

What actually happens in the seventy-two hours after a man dies in Pakistan — the washing, the
cloth, the shoulder, the plates, the phone that keeps ringing, the room that gets emptied — told
flat, as a report, by someone who has watched it happen too many times.

---

## Why this one, and not another story

It hits the two engines that actually spread, at the same time, without being cruel:

| engine | where it fires | rate |
|---|---|---|
| **AWE** | the speed and coldness of the machine: ghusl, kafan, janazah, mitti — **no coffin, no delay, buried inside 24 hours** | 5.3× |
| **ANGER** | the plates. The men who never called arrive first and stay longest, and the ones who ate at his table are the ones who count the rice | 4.8× |
| **ANXIETY** | the phone still rings. Your number is still in the group. Then someone removes it | 3.7× |
| **NOT sadness** | the film never asks you to cry. It asks you to *check* | 0.7× refused |

And it passes the only test that matters for a share: **what does a person say about themselves by
sending this?** → *"I'm the one who thinks about this honestly. I'm not pretending."* Flattering.
That is Berger's social currency, earned without a lie.

## The seven moves

1. **HOOK (question, 2 lines).** *Tumhare marne ke baad teen din hote hain. Tumhe pata hai un teen din
   me kya hota hai?* No greeting, no wind-up. A question, then silence.
2. **DIN 1 — the machine.** Ghusl. Kafan. No coffin. No embalming. Janazah. Mitti. All inside
   twenty-four hours. Facts only, spoken slowly, while the picture shows hands and water and cloth.
   **Awe is not a feeling here, it is a timetable.**
3. **The shoulder.** Who carries him. The names that arrive are the names that were absent for years.
   Nobody mentions this. The film does.
4. **DIN 2 — the plates.** Food is brought by the community; that is documented practice. Who cooks,
   who eats, who counts, who leaves early and takes a plate. His own sentence, already in his script:
   *"wohi apne aate hain aur mayyat par khane ka intezam karte hain."* **This is the anger beat.**
5. **DIN 3 — the phone.** Hidad ends at three days. The phone still rings. His number is still in the
   group. Then it isn't. Somebody removed a man in one tap.
6. **The room.** Clothes, shoes, phone, the chair, the spectacles. Distributed inside a week. Nobody
   asks him. Nobody can.
7. **The last mention.** The chaleeswa. His name is said out loud for the last time by people who came
   for the food. Then he becomes a date on a paper in a drawer.

## The moral — TABAHI level

> **Teen din ke baad jo tumhara naam bachta hai, woh us aadmi ke paas hota hai jise tumne bachaya tha.
> Uske paas nahi jo tumhare ghar me khana khane aaya tha.**

Why it lands:
- It arrives as **arithmetic**, not comfort. Three days, one survivor, one name.
- It is **angry without insulting anyone**. The enemy is a behaviour everybody recognises from their
  own family's funeral.
- It is **shareable as identity**: sending it says *I'm not one of the plate people.*
- It is **true to his own material**. The plate sentence is his. I did not invent the wound, I only
  built the room around it.

---

## The rules this film has to obey

1. **Never about Allah. Always about people.** No afterlife claims, no religious rulings, no fear
   sermon. The camera stays on the living — who came, who ate, who left, who forgot. The moment it
   becomes a religious lecture, the anger dies and the shares die with it.
2. **Every fact is checkable.** Ghusl, kafan, no coffin, burial within 24 hours, hidad of three days,
   community bringing food — all documented practice, not my invention. Anything I cannot source does
   not enter the film. That rule has already cost me two versions; it stays.
3. **Third person until the last line.** The film is about *him*, a man we never name. "Tum" appears
   only in the hook and the moral — that is what keeps the you/I ratio near 1.9 instead of 2.4.
4. **Sentence length 12–14 words.** Not fragments. Complete thoughts. The gate enforces it.
5. **It ends on an object, not a lesson.** The lesson is spoken once, then a drawer closes.

## Why this can create a trend, not one video

The format is a **container**: *"TEEN DIN"* → any life event measured in a fixed number of days.
- `TEEN DIN` — after death
- `TEEN DIN` — after a job loss
- `TEEN DIN` — after he stopped calling
- `TEEN DIN` — after she left

Same skeleton, same cold report, same moral structure, different 72 hours. One template, infinite
episodes, one brand: **TABAHI**. That is what a trend is — a shape other people can copy without
asking permission.
