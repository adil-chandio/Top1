## Executive finding

ExtinctZoo's current market is not explained by one “viral title.” The current
official inventory shows several repeatable editorial packages:

1. **Mental-model reversal:** an animal, place, or time interval is not what the
   audience assumes — for example, extinct animals that never were, amphibians as
   apex predators, or a “boring” billion that was not boring.
2. **Progressive evidence ladder:** a concrete fossil, anatomical feature, site,
   map, or climate proxy appears early, then the film widens from the clue to a
   mechanism and a larger consequence.
3. **List/franchise progression:** “every period” and “from each period” create
   multiple payoff checkpoints and make 20–40 minutes feel like a journey.
4. **High-stakes but legible language:** largest, deadliest, tragic, brutal,
   never, and extinct are emotionally clear, but most of these words are already
   saturated on this channel.

The most defensible opportunity for a new Extinct film is therefore **not another
largest/deadliest/strangest compilation**. It is a specific evidence-led reversal
that can be proved with original diagrams, maps, specimens, or licensed/public-
domain visual material, while stating what remains uncertain.

This is an editorial hypothesis. Displayed views are observations at unequal ages;
they do not establish CTR, retention, recommendation mechanics, causation, or a
future forecast.

## Opening promise and story progression

The watch-page transcripts expose a consistent sequence. It is not a fixed script,
but it recurs often enough to be a useful editorial observation:

### 1. Contradiction or emotional frame

- `tMOsrsKAuhg`: the supposed extinct animals could not have existed as presented;
  the opening case is a giant marine monster assembled from mismatched fossils.
- `aIXEjUl57jE`: harmless-present-day amphibians once included apex predators.
- `q33JeQ3ZxXs`: a billion-year interval called boring may have been consequential.
- `NF42pH3oXZw`: every fossil is already a death record, but some deaths are much
  more tragic.

The premise is understandable before the specialist names arrive. That is likely
more repeatable than any particular adjective.

### 2. Concrete evidence arrives before the film becomes a catalogue

Examples from the fetched transcripts:

- synchrotron imaging, a sealed burrow, co-preserved animals, healed ribs, and
  bite marks in `NF42pH3oXZw`;
- mammal-only teeth and five separate Basilosaurus fossils in `tMOsrsKAuhg`;
- projectile wounds and arrowheads at Jebel Sahaba in `nuvZp7DIMTk`;
- period fossils and size metrics in `yLtz7G2e0cI`;
- Chicxulub crater, asteroid composition, speed, and impact physics in
  `It8orOogiPI`.

The evidence is used as a ladder: visible clue → interpretation → setting or
mechanism → broader consequence → uncertainty/scope correction.

### 3. A sequence supplies the middle

The strongest list rows do not merely enumerate names. They move through periods,
scale, and ecological roles. `eWu92oXtxsw` even publishes period chapter times
from Cambrian through present. `fKizW6NlnoA` makes the absence of land predators
in early periods part of the progression rather than hiding it.

### 4. Scope corrections protect the promise

`Ig39JrUPi90` rejects the Great Dying as a too-easy “deadliest” answer before
choosing a sustained Late Triassic ecological danger. `uH-hucq-dmA` flags the
uncertain flying status of Rhyniognatha. These caveats make the story more
credible, even when the title uses a superlative.

## Title grammar across the official inventory

### Reusable

- **Impossible status:** “never were,” “shouldn't exist,” “not a single water
  dinosaur.”
- **Role reversal:** “amphibians were apex predators,” “prey became scarier than
  predators,” “evolution made the same animal twice.”
- **Familiar place/time reversal:** Antarctica before freezing, Earth iceless,
  Amazon as a mega lake, Australia with a sea.
- **Structured comparison:** “from every period,” “every single period,” “all 5
  types.”
- **Specific evidence mystery:** rare fossils, preserved injuries, strange mouths,
  or a hidden archaeological clue.

### Saturated or unsafe to copy literally

- generic “deadliest/largest/strangest” claims without a defined comparison set;
- repeated prehistoric-human death and injury anthologies;
- another period-by-period predator list;
- another familiar geography reversal that is only “place X used to be green/wet”; 
- a copied “Never Were,” “Apex Predators,” or “Before It Froze” title shape;
- any title that turns a disputed inference into a fact;
- any package whose visual proof requires unlicensed paleoart or a sourced image
  that cannot be cleared.

## Thumbnail audit and rights gate

The official watch pages exposed exact-ID image endpoints for the 20 detailed rows,
for example:

`https://i.ytimg.com/vi/NF42pH3oXZw/maxresdefault.jpg`

and the corresponding `vi_webp` URL. The endpoint strings are recorded in the
JSON ledger. **The pixels were not accepted as inspected evidence in this pass**:
the sandbox could not retrieve the i.ytimg.com image bytes through its direct/file
path, while image search returned Reddit reposts, third-party podcast artwork, or
generic thumbnail pages. Those results are deliberately excluded.

Therefore:

- no exact thumbnail focal subject, text count, crop, contrast, or phone-size
  conclusion is promoted;
- no ExtinctZoo artwork, font, composition, paleoart, footage, music, or narration
  is being copied;
- the rights/tracing discussion visible in Reddit search results is only a
  provenance warning, not an infringement finding;
- official pixel inspection remains an open gate before any thumbnail grammar is
  used in production.

## Market gap to carry into topic generation

The gap is narrow and testable:

> **An original, evidence-first reversal in which one named physical clue changes a
> familiar assumption, with the claim scoped to what the evidence can actually
> support.**

A viable candidate should have:

1. one ordinary assumption a general viewer can understand;
2. one surprising but defensible reversal;
3. a primary source or authoritative institutional record;
4. a visual proof path that does not depend on competitor art or unknown rights;
5. a mechanism and consequence that can sustain a long-form arc;
6. an explicit uncertainty/limitation beat;
7. no direct overlap with ExtinctZoo's recurring period-list, death-list, or
   familiar-place packages.

The next step is to derive 3–5 **new** candidate topics from that gap, run the
primary-source, claim, rights, visual-proof, feasibility, and weighted-score gates,
and stop at owner selection. No candidate is accepted by this report.
