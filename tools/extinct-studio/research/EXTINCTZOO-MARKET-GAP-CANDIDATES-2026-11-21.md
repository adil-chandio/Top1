## How these candidates were derived

The official ExtinctZoo audit found a reusable but crowded grammar: make a
familiar assumption fail, introduce a concrete physical clue early, then widen
from evidence to mechanism and consequence. The saturated lanes are generic
largest/deadliest/strangest claims, period-by-period predator lists, prehistoric
human death compilations, and familiar-place reversals without a new evidence
question.

These five candidates instead use one primary fossil or trace as the center of a
long-form investigation. They are not copied ExtinctZoo titles, thumbnails, art,
footage, music, narration, or exact hooks.

## Primary-source and claim gates

### 1. Trilobite last meal

- **Primary source:** Kraft et al., *Nature* (2023), DOI
  [10.1038/s41586-023-06567-7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06567-7).
- **What it supports:** an Ordovician *Bohemolichas incola* preserved in a
  siliceous nodule; complete/itemized gut contents; synchrotron microtomography;
  fragmented calcareous shells; a neutral or alkaline gut inference.
- **Claim boundary:** this is one unusually preserved specimen. It does not
  establish that all trilobites scavenged or that a pH value was directly measured
  in a living animal.
- **Visual proof:** original cutaway and shell-fragment animation; no Nature
  figure or scan reuse.

### 2. Dinocephalosaurus live birth

- **Primary source:** Liu et al., *Nature Communications* (2017), DOI
  [10.1038/ncomms14445](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms14445).
- **What it supports:** a pregnant *Dinocephalosaurus* from about 245 Ma; embryo
  enclosed in the maternal body; evidence for live birth in Archosauromorpha; a
  phylogenetic model concerning genotypic sex determination.
- **Claim boundary:** the embryo is incomplete and the sex-determination result
  is modelled, not directly read from chromosomes. Do not call every archosaur a
  live-bearer or make the animal a direct bird/crocodile ancestor.
- **Visual proof:** original rib-cage/embryo diagram and alternative-explanation
  board.

### 3. Permian regurgitalite

- **Primary source:** *Scientific Reports* (published 2026-01-30), DOI
  [10.1038/s41598-025-33381-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-33381-0).
- **What it supports:** an early Permian Bromacker bone cluster of 41 elements;
  micro-CT, osteological, chemical, and taphonomic analysis; a regurgitalite
  interpretation; possible producers including *Dimetrodon teutonis* or
  *Tambacarnifex unguifalcatus*.
- **Claim boundary:** the producer is unresolved. Do not state that Dimetrodon
  definitely made it or that every named animal was eaten in one confirmed meal.
- **Visual proof:** native bone-cluster diagram, digestive-product distinction,
  and a branching food web that keeps the producer uncertain.

### 4. Cretaceous deep-sea fish traces

- **Primary source:** *PNAS* (2023), DOI
  [10.1073/pnas.2306164120](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2306164120).
- **What it supports:** fish-feeding trails and pits in Early Cretaceous abyssal
  plain deposits of northwest Italy; about 130 Ma; traces consistent with modern
  demersal feeding; at least three fish species inferred; early deep-seafloor
  vertebrate colonization in the paper's comparison.
- **Claim boundary:** no exact fish body is identified. The feeding mechanism is
  inferred by trace comparison, and “earliest” depends on the defined fossil
  record and age control.
- **Visual proof:** original slab-to-trace cross-section, Tethys map, and a
  body-fossil versus trace-fossil limitation card.

### 5. Kryoryctes and the platypus-like lifestyle

- **Primary source:** Hand et al., *PNAS* (2025), DOI
  [10.1073/pnas.2413569122](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2413569122).
- **What it supports:** an Early Cretaceous *Kryoryctes cadburyi* humerus from
  about 108–103 Ma; overall shape closer to echidnas but internal microstructure
  with semiaquatic specializations; stem-monotreme placement; inference of a
  semiaquatic burrowing lifestyle.
- **Claim boundary:** the animal is known from a single humerus and is not a
  direct modern platypus ancestor. The lifestyle is a probabilistic comparative
  inference, not an observed behavior.
- **Visual proof:** original bone cross-section, comparative silhouettes, and a
  time tree that does not imply direct ancestry.

## Rights gate

All five are **native-diagram-only research candidates**. The primary pages expose
figures, but open access or a journal page does not automatically grant permission
to reuse a figure, specimen photo, scan, or reconstruction in a commercial video.
The production-safe default is:

- cite the paper and redraw the mechanism from the published claims;
- use no journal figure, source video, competitor thumbnail, or unverified paleoart;
- verify any external map, silhouette, specimen, or sound asset before use;
- keep source media out of the repository until its license is documented.

Thus “eligible for owner review” does **not** mean “cleared for production.”
Unknown or pending rights remain a hard production blocker.
