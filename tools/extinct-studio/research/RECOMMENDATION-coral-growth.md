# Recommendation for Extinct Long 01

## Recommended candidate

**A Coral's Life in Cross-Section**  
Research ID: `extinct-long-001-coral-growth`  
Status: **recommended for primary-source verification; not yet owner-selected or production-ready**

This is the strongest of the fresh candidates for a first long-form film. It is a
recommendation about editorial potential, not a claim that it will go viral.

## Why this one wins

### 1. The first frame can show a real mystery

A thin section can begin as an abstract pattern, then resolve into the internal
architecture of an extinct Silurian rugose coral. That gives the viewer an
immediate visual question: **what is this pattern, and how did it form?** It is
stronger than opening with a generic prehistoric landscape or a talking-head
explanation.

### 2. It has a native cinematic transformation

The film can move through a controlled chain:

```text
thin section → repeated growth structure → 3D coral reconstruction → lost seafloor context
```

The transformation is an explanation of evidence, not a replacement for it. It
also gives the renderer real work: macro surface, cross-section, growth-series
continuity, light through material, scale reveal, and a final environment shot.

### 3. It is less crowded than dinosaur and megafauna hooks

The channel can still speak about extinction and lost life, but the object is
less familiar. “A fossil coral kept a growth record” is a different promise from
another predator chase or generic ancient-world flyover. Novelty helps the title
and thumbnail earn a click without inventing danger or exaggerating the science.

### 4. It supports a complete long-form arc

A possible editorial arc is:

1. show the pattern before naming it;
2. reveal the fossil and its age after primary-source verification;
3. explain what a thin section preserves;
4. follow a growth series instead of pretending the fossil is a time-lapse;
5. reconstruct only the anatomy/environment supported by the evidence;
6. separate observed structure from modelled colour, motion, and habitat;
7. end with what the fossil can tell us and what it cannot.

### 5. It can produce future formats without cropping the long film

After the long film is accepted, separate native Shorts could focus on:

- the first reveal of the cross-section;
- how a growth series is read;
- what a fossil slice cannot prove.

Those are independent Shorts, not automatic crops or release promises.

## Why it is not cleared yet

The public GitHub README describes 225 high-resolution scans and says CC BY 4.0,
but the repository's GitHub license metadata is unknown. The source repository is
therefore a **research lead**, not a cleared media pack. The original Wrzołek and
Zatoń 2023 publication, supplement, dataset terms, specimen context, and exact
claims must be read before the script or picture lock.

Until that gate passes:

- do not download or ship the scans as production plates;
- do not call the dataset commercial-use cleared;
- do not state an exact age, growth rate, ecology, or anatomy beyond the verified
  source;
- do not animate a generic reef as if it were the specimen's actual habitat;
- use original schematic/3D work only after the claim boundaries are locked.

## Viral potential — honest version

This candidate has the **highest viral-readiness potential** in the current fresh
shortlist because it combines:

- a visually unusual first frame;
- a simple human question;
- a satisfying scale change;
- a real evidence object;
- a clear before/after understanding;
- a distinctive thumbnail silhouette;
- a strong long-form-to-Shorts family.

It still has risks:

- the subject may be too unfamiliar without a clean hook;
- microscopic detail can fail on a phone;
- the science can become abstract if the growth mechanism is not visibly staged;
- rights ambiguity can delay or kill the visual treatment;
- no score can predict YouTube distribution.

The internal score is **79/100**, below the 80 owner-review threshold only because
rights certainty is not yet high enough. That is the correct failure mode: fix the
source/rights gate rather than inflate the score.

## Next production gate

Before a long script exists:

1. recover and read the primary paper and supplement;
2. verify the dataset license/terms independently;
3. build the claim and limitation ledger;
4. make a rights-safe visual reference board;
5. design the 8–12 second native proof;
6. rerun the idea score;
7. wait for owner selection.

Only then should the project move from research-only to an actual Extinct Studio
project directory. No historical release candidate has been reopened.
