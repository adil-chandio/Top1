## Viewer question

How can one 465-million-year-old trilobite fossil reveal what it ate and how its
digestive system worked, even though no living behavior was observed?

## Primary paper receipt

**Kraft, P. et al.** “Uniquely preserved gut contents illuminate trilobite
palaeophysiology.” *Nature* (2023).  
DOI: `10.1038/s41586-023-06567-7`  
Official article: <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06567-7>

The paper reports an Ordovician *Bohemolichas incola* preserved in a siliceous
nodule from the Šárka Formation of the Prague Basin, Czech Republic. The authors
used propagation phase-contrast synchrotron microtomography to study the specimen.
The article says the gut fill is three-dimensional, nearly continuous, and made
up of partly fragmented calcareous shells.

## What the paper directly gives us

1. **A specimen:** an articulated *Bohemolichas incola* fossil, approximately
   465 million years old.
2. **A physical clue:** shell-rich material is preserved through the digestive
   tract, not merely beside the fossil.
3. **Identifiable contents:** ostracod valves, a hyolith conch and operculum,
   likely stylophoran echinoderm plates, probable thin-walled bivalve fragments,
   and other shell fragments.
4. **Anatomical reconstruction:** the paper reconstructs a spacious, J-shaped
   digestive tract with a two-chambered foregut/crop and a wider intestine.
5. **Food-processing evidence:** the distribution and condition of fragments are
   consistent with a light-crushing, size/resistance-limited feeding strategy;
   the paper interprets the animal as predominantly an opportunistic scavenger.
6. **Physiology inference:** undissolved sharp-edged calcareous shells throughout
   the gut support an alkaline or nearly neutral internal environment, because
   an acid environment would be expected to etch the shells.

## Claim boundaries

- The study is one exceptional specimen, not a census of trilobite behavior.
- “Last meal” is a viewer-facing shorthand for preserved gut contents; it does
  not prove the exact moment of feeding before death.
- “Scavenger” is the authors' ecological interpretation, not a directly observed
  behavior. Use “predominantly opportunistic scavenger” with attribution and
  preserve uncertainty.
- The near-neutral/alkaline gut claim is inferred from shell preservation and
  compared with living arthropods. It is not a direct pH measurement from a living
  trilobite.
- “A 465-million-year-old stomach scan” is imprecise. The evidence is a fossilized
  specimen and a synchrotron scan model; do not imply the original soft tissue is
  intact as living tissue.
- The paper discusses possible digestive glands and mechanical processing, but
  those structures are reconstructions and analogies. They need explicit
  “inferred,” “probably,” or “may have” language.

## Story spine, not a script

1. **Familiar assumption:** trilobites are usually presented as flat armored
   fossils with little visible behavior.
2. **Promise:** one fossil preserves a meal inside the animal, not just a shell
   beside it.
3. **Reveal:** original diagrams place shell fragments along a continuous gut
   corridor and identify several prey/food elements.
4. **Mechanism:** the paper's reconstruction explains the two-chambered foregut,
   shell resistance, and movement of material through the intestine.
5. **Bigger implication:** the shell fragments provide a route from fossil anatomy
   to feeding behavior and digestive chemistry.
6. **Limitation:** one specimen can illuminate a behavior without defining every
   trilobite; the ecology remains an inference bounded by the paper.
