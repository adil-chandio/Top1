# Extinct Life — Atlantic Tracks: science and visual checks

**18 November 2026 · Drafting gate passed.** The sources support the track-locality comparison and a proposed land corridor, not a documented journey. The [English script v1](Extinct-Life-Atlantic-Tracks-Script-v1.md) and [opening board](../../../deliverables/extinct-life-step-2/Extinct-Life-Atlantic-Tracks-Opening-Board.png) are for review. The opening works with the cleared map data alone. Specimen-photo rights are not a dependency for the draft and have not been cleared for the final film.

## What changed after checking the paper

The 2024 chapter compares **regions** and their rock records. It says that point-by-point comparison of the tracks had not been done and that the available dates do not establish that the separate layers are the same age. It does **not** demonstrate hundreds of paired prints. Its account of **262** individual marks is for **KB-17 in Cameroon** (54 trackways in Congleton's earlier account), not a shared inventory of matched pairs across countries.

There is also a newer source worth using: **Menkem et al.**, *Journal of Palaeogeography*, available online 22 August 2026 as a journal pre-proof. It examines **283 prints in 34 documented trackways across four surfaces at KB-17**, compares preservation with Brazil's Sousa Basin, and supports the corridor hypothesis. It reports an approximately **69 cm** three-toed print. Those counts describe a particular survey and are not added to the earlier counts or used to infer population size. The draft uses “hundreds of prints,” the measured length and the new sediment evidence for microbial mats helping to preserve some impressions.

| C2 · Distances | About **6,000 km** apart now; about **1,000 km** apart on one ancient landmass in the study's reconstruction. | J24 abstract and conclusions. Geographical approximations, not measured trackway lengths. The two board panels use the same degree scale. The board's independent 125 Ma model check gives roughly 6,085 km present and 1,194 km reconstructed between the chosen **regional** anchors. It is not a recalculation of J24's quoted numbers or a measurement of a route. |
| C3 · Age and opening | Early Cretaceous, more than 120 million years ago in the study's broad framing. Rifting and separation progressed over time; the relevant land was still connected when these tracks were laid down. | J24 discussion, limits and conclusions, incl. fig. 7. M26 gives a likely Berriasian–Hauterivian assignment for Koum and discusses subsequent stages of separation. Do not date the formation of the whole Atlantic to one day, the map label to an individual print, or the two beds to the same instant. |
| C4 · What a shape says | Both regions have three-toed tracks, many interpreted as theropod. Substrate can change what a footprint looks like. | J24 Koum and discussion sections; fig. 5 gives Koum examples **KB-3 footprint 3** and **KB-17 footprint 5-3**, not a cross-ocean pair. M26 describes how sediments and preservation influence morphology. Do not use a partial press photo to diagnose a species, speed, or an individual; the proposed stamp demo is explicitly illustrative. |
| C5 · Four layers and one large print | KB-17 preserves four different track-bearing surfaces. In the later investigation, impression **TK-034** is ~69 cm long and consistent with a large theropod. | J24 figs. 3–5 and M26 abstract, highlights and track-map captions. These are measurements of the mark, not the animal's height. Do not infer an exact body length, a species, a single herd, or an exact time gap between levels. Fig. 5B in J24 is a different ID from TK-034; don't present either press picture as the 69 cm specimen. |
| C6 · Sediment and age clues | Ripples and mud cracks support alternating wet and dry conditions at Koum. Thin sections and surface textures suggest microbial mats helped stabilize the track-bearing sediment. Fault-bounded basins accumulated river/lake deposits; regional fossils help bracket their age. | M26 highlights, abstract and figs. 5 and 8; J24 basin stratigraphy, pollen and discussion. The useful pollen correlations involve regional beds including other Cameroon basins, not a clock attached to each track. J24 notes a limited Koum pollen record. J24's broader climate model and M26's local reading have different scopes, so the draft doesn't depict these basins as a modern tropical rainforest. |
| C7 · Corridor and rivers | The geography plus local evidence supports a **possible** zone of dispersal. River valleys may have helped connect suitable habitats. | J24 “Rivers as Dispersal Routes,” fig. 7, discussion, conclusions; M26 abstract. This is an inference about opportunities for animals to move, not a uniquely traced path, a direction of a journey, proof of the same species, or proof that any specific animal went between the sites. |
| C8 · Why the distance changed | Relative movement of the plates ultimately separated the regions as the South Atlantic opened and widened. Tracks remain with the rock that holds them. | J24 conclusions and M26 section 2.1; [USGS plate-tectonics history](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/historical.html) for the broader mechanism. Don't animate modern coastlines as exact cuts, or depict a visible tear racing beneath a dinosaur. |

## Proof and next gate

The 1920×1080 opening board was rendered and checked at full resolution and 768×432. Check first-frame legibility again in the cut; these two views are separate snapshots, not a time-resolved reconstruction. The [render receipt](opening-board-receipt.json) records source and image checks. To rebuild, restore the three pinned `inputs` from [map-sources.json](map-sources.json) into a local directory under their listed filenames. Run `python3 tools/extinct-life/step-2/build_opening_board.py --sources <that-directory>` from the repo root (Python 3.11+, Pillow 11.3.0, pyshp 2.3.1, DejaVu fonts; `--fonts` overrides the path). It checks input SHA-256 before rendering and does not require GPlates or OpenGL.

Read the script aloud and resolve any phrasing/visual mismatch before timing VO. At lock, confirm the photo option or the map-first fallback and the visible source credit. No voice audition, narration, scored animatic, CGI, edit, upload, or Greenland change in this stage.
