## Channel direction

**Extinct Life investigates ancient animals through what they left behind.** Open with one strange piece of evidence, show what changed the scientists’ interpretation, and distinguish the finding from the reconstruction. Use the existing English-led documentary style. Keep the narration warm and clear, and let the evidence make the hook rather than an exaggerated claim.

**Possible About line:** “A tooth, a footprint, a fragment of DNA. Extinct Life follows the clues that survived to find out what we can still know about lost worlds.”

## New film in development — The Fish With a Spiral of Teeth

**Subject:** *Helicoprion*. **Format:** approximately 5 minutes, English, 16:9. **Premise:** A fossil spiral tells us about the teeth, but on its own it doesn’t tell us where they fit. The story follows the preserved cartilage in one specimen to the lower jaw.

This is a separate story from the archive’s Greenland master, mammoth review reel, and Livyatan test. The viewer gets the answer to the first puzzle — where the whorl fits — then a second question to carry the film: how could one mouth keep adding teeth while retaining old ones?

### Opening draft · 0:00–0:28

| Time | English narration | Picture and sound |

| **0:00–0:05** | “This spiral is made of teeth.” | First frame: close on the points, then pull back enough to read the entire whorl. One dry rock-detail sound; no logo or bass hit. |
| **0:05–0:12** | “It belonged to a fish called Helicoprion. But where did the teeth fit?” | Keep the specimen visible. Show the name once and leave the missing outline blank rather than presenting a speculative animal as evidence. |
| **0:12–0:20** | “A scan revealed cartilage beside the spiral. The teeth ran down the middle of the lower jaw.” | Original cutaway graphic. Map the preserved cartilage, then place the whorl in the jaw without making the teeth spin. Label the graphic **CT-based reconstruction**. |
| **0:20–0:28** | “Now there’s a second puzzle: how do you keep adding teeth without losing the old ones?” | Distinguish younger and older teeth with shape and colour. Bridge into the evidence sequence with the same low room tone. |

### Main sequence

| Target | What the viewer learns | Visual proof |

### Evidence and production checks

- **What is supported:** the CT reconstruction places the whorl through the mandibular arch, supported along the lower jaw’s midline. Helicoprion was an extinct cartilaginous fish on the holocephalan side of the family tree, not a modern shark. [Tapanila et al., *Biology Letters* (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2013.0057).
- **What is modelled:** a proposed feeding mechanism for opening and closing the jaw, including how it might have worked on soft-bodied prey. Do not show a motor-like rotating blade or specify a confirmed prey species. [Ramsay et al., *Journal of Morphology* (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1002/jmor.20319).
- **What stays provisional:** full body shape, colour and exact motion. Draw original explanatory graphics; check the rights of any specimen photo before including it. Label any new animal shot **Reconstruction**.
- **Narration:** check the opening read against the approved long-film vocal standard before animating the full timeline. Match tone and listening level; no harsh presence boost to make it feel louder. Set the read, then spot a small number of Foley accents. Check low-volume phone and earbuds; no long empty gaps.
- **First animation pass:** an 8–12 second review proof of fossil → cartilage → lower jaw, using one stable angle to make the fit readable. Verify the geometry, then spot a short narration read and restrained Foley to it before user review. Do not start the full render or thumbnail until the story direction is settled.
