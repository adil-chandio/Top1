# Extinct Life — Batch 01: Market research and Top 3

**18 November 2026 · Topic selected: the Brazil–Cameroon dinosaur footprints.**

**Direction:** An English-language documentary series that starts with a real clue—a tooth, a track, a layer of rock—and shows how it changes what we know about an extinct animal or lost ecosystem. A reconstruction should make the evidence easier to understand, not stand in for it.

**Selected: #1, the Atlantic dinosaur tracks.** The owner delegated the topic choice. Of the three treatments, this has the clearest first-frame premise for a general audience: recognisable dinosaur tracks, separated by an ocean today, followed by the reveal of their positions on the same ancient landmass. That is an editorial judgement about shareability, not a view prediction. Helicoprion and Cerro Ballena are backups, not parallel productions.

## 3. What to use for Extinct Life

1. **Start with one surprising, legible thing.** Eons' Antarctica outline and the two sails are different ways to make the question concrete. ExtinctZoo's higher reversal gives another possible entry point. Our version should let a viewer recognise the evidence itself: two distinct tracks, a fossil spiral, or four bone-bearing layers. Avoid copying the competitors' art, fonts, logos, “deadliest” claims or aggressive look.
2. **Let title and cover do different jobs without promising different stories.** One asks the question; the other shows the clue or stakes. Eons' Trees example warns against turning a narrower climbing question into an unrelated origin claim. The lower rows can have sharp imagery and bold type as well. No evidence here that arrows, type, fear, or a particular phrase causes more views. After the story is chosen, check the same composition at phone size with no headline and with one short label. Judge whether the object and question are readable before testing any claim about clicks.
3. **Use a change we can show.** Compare an ancient and modern map, add the cartilage layer back to a whorl, or separate a misleadingly single-looking bone bed into levels. These are tests for whether the explanation communicates; not a requirement to render spectacular animal action. Large established channels can carry broad worlds and longer runtimes; the sample does not establish the best runtime, upload timing or audience size for Extinct Life.

**Evidence limit:** Lifetime views reflect exposure over time. We do not know impressions, CTR, retention, traffic sources, tests, audience overlap or promotion. Publishers can edit titles and thumbnails; an indexed cover is one verified version, not a record of everything viewers saw. All rankings are descriptive with small, non-random samples and residual differences in age, subject and distribution. No view or monetization promise.

## 4. Ranked treatments

These are working concept names, not final title/thumbnail copy or cold opens. The ranking weighs how clearly we can show the evidence, how distinct the treatment is from existing coverage, and how much reconstruction and asset work it would need. “Original” refers to our visual treatment and emphasis, not to being the first to cover the fossil. Topic demand for these treatments, figure rights, and whether the hardest visual can be executed accurately are all still untested.

### 1 · Why These Dinosaur Footprints Are an Ocean Apart — selected

- **Question and visual:** Similar three-toed tracks are preserved in Brazil and Cameroon. Put two distinct track shapes on screen, then rewind the map: today's sites are about 6,000 km apart, and the paper reconstructs them about 1,000 km apart on the same landmass. The payoff is how a traversable landscape can be inferred even though there is no continuous trackway to follow. The map makes the modern/ancient contrast as concrete as Eons' Antarctic outline, but the two fossil traces anchor it so it is not just a broad habitat reversal.
- **Evidence:** [Jacobs et al., New Mexico Museum of Natural History and Science Bulletin 95 (2024)](https://igeo.ufrj.br/inc/isc/3/03_139_Jacobs%20etal.pdf) uses track-bearing sediments, age, and basin structure to propose a pre-Aptian dispersal corridor. **Not** the same individual, the same identified species, a single continuous trail, or a dinosaur walking through an existing Atlantic. The approximate palaeodistance is an estimate from the reconstruction. Check the exact figure and map provenance before production.
- **Original angle / risk:** [WION covered the exact comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJRv3OEdoYU) as a news podcast, and [ScienceAlert covered it at publication](https://www.sciencealert.com/matching-dinosaur-footprints-found-3700-miles-apart-reveal-earths-past). Eons already has a [fossils-and-tectonics film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BMJJoAtvHiY) about Cambrian trilobites and an ocean that closed, and a [dinosaur-trackway episode](https://www.pbs.org/video/the-story-of-the-dino-stampede-hlzgfb/) on Lark Quarry in Australia. The difference here is the Early Cretaceous case, and showing how location, approximate age and the rocks support a passage rather than simply saying the footprints “match.” The search of study/site names and the three direct channels was not exhaustive. Low-to-moderate visual load if built around an original map and two accurately sourced, distinct track outlines. Avoid a “same tracks” or “crossed the ocean” promise.

### 2 · The Fish That Kept Its Old Teeth — Helicoprion

- **Question and visual:** How could a fish add new teeth while keeping old ones in a spiral? Use a consistent specimen view and make the preserved cartilage and tooth sequence legible. Like Eons' Sails example, the strange structure can carry the image without a generic monster close-up. ApexMorph's lower-view “evil twin” listing is a reason not to sell it as another Megalodon.
- **Evidence:** [Tapanila et al., *Biology Letters* (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2013.0057) uses CT to locate the whorl along the lower-jaw midline and describes continued tooth addition and retention. [Ramsay et al., *Journal of Morphology* (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1002/jmor.20319) models possible feeding. Helicoprion was a cartilaginous fish on the holocephalan side, not a modern shark. Separate CT-based anatomy, possible feeding motion and unknown body appearance; do not depict a spinning saw or a confirmed prey species.
- **Original angle / risk:** PBS Eons already covered the placement-and-scan puzzle as a [4:15 standalone in 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S5GHg27wYLI) and revisited it at [5:46 in the November Pangea episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yGf78wc6jIU&t=346s). So “where was the saw?” on its own is not distinct, and the idea that teeth were retained is not new. Trace the different-sized teeth in a single specimen and show what their order can establish without claiming an exact growth rate or age. Best single-object CGI option; close and recent direct topic overlap. Rights and geometry not checked at asset level yet.

### 3 · The Desert With Four Layers of Whale Fossils — Cerro Ballena

- **Question and visual:** Why would one stretch of desert contain whale fossils at four different rock levels? Show a road-cut cross-section where the levels can be read one by one. The human-scale setting and the whale skeleton offer an immediate contrast; the distinct horizons, not a generic ancient landscape, are the payoff. This tests the same concrete-clue approach as Eons' [Solnhofen lagoon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wiBgx05-JJs) story about why a fossil site can mislead, without retelling that episode.
- **Evidence:** [Pyenson et al., *Proceedings B* (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.3316) documented four fossil-bearing horizons at this Late Miocene site in Chile. Arrangement and preservation support repeated accumulations, with deaths at sea and burial on a former tidal flat inferred. Harmful algal blooms are a proposed explanation, not something directly measured in the ancient animals. This is not a claim that all whales were killed at once or that the events caused a species' extinction.
- **Original angle / risk:** [Eons' 2020 episode](https://www.pbs.org/video/how-the-andes-mountains-might-have-killed-a-bunch-of-whales-ye2ur1/) explicitly covers the four layers, transport and the algae explanation. A different treatment would have to do more than retell those findings: build the explanation around a cross-section and use the skeletons' intactness and belly-up orientation to separate the deposition evidence from the mortality hypothesis. This is a different visual emphasis, not an unserved story. Moderate visual load: accurate horizons and representative skeletons matter more than a full ocean reconstruction. Verify the specimen/scan rights and avoid implying a connection to the archived Livyatan test.

**Not on this shortlist: the Paris Biota.** [Brayard et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1602159) supports a surprisingly complex **local** marine community in what is now Idaho about 1.3 million years after the end-Permian extinction, not proof that the oceans everywhere had recovered. [Eons covers Paris Canyon in its 2026 recovery episode](https://www.pbs.org/video/who-survived-the-great-dying-and-why-life-and-death-on-pangea-pzs3ev/). The scope and number of organisms to explain make this less practical for the first evidence-led film.

## Decision and next gate

**Proceed with the Atlantic tracks only.** The specific case was already covered in news, but the closest Eons titles found concern different fossils. Tracks plus an ocean and a map rewind communicate the premise faster than a specialised tooth-growth question or a four-layer bone bed. The key check is whether two distinct track outlines and their geographic context can support a clear, accurate comparison. `6,000 KM APART` is a working cover cue for the approximate modern distance, not approved art.
